### Critical

**2.1 — Fabricated citation.**
`Kervezee, L., Shechter, A., & Boivin, D. B. (2022). Impact of shift work on the metabolic
syndrome and diabetes. Frontiers in Public Health, 10, Art. 1034195.`

No such publication exists. Checked Kervezee's complete PubMed record (40 items) and Crossref.
The DOI in the reference list, `10.3389/fpubh.2022.1034195`, belongs to **Boini, Bourgkard,
Ferrières & Esquirol (2022), "What do we know about the effect of night-shift work on
cardiovascular risk factors? An umbrella review."** A panelist who clicks the DOI lands on a
different paper by four different authors. Cited twice, Ch. II lines 55 and 61. It is not
logged in `docs/reference-integration.md` at all.

**2.2 — A number contradicted by its own source.**
The paper: *"SWSD, affecting nearly 40% of night shift workers (Boivin & Boudreau, 2014)."*
That source states: *"a shift work sleep disorder can be found in **2–5%** of workers."*
A ~10× discrepancy. The anxiety/depression/chronic-fatigue association attributed to it is
also not in that source. (Figures near 30–40% exist in the literature — Drake et al. 2004 —
but not in this reference.)

**2.3 — Wrong outcome variable.** "25–38% greater likelihood of **obesity**". Boini reports
those figures for **overweight**; obesity is 5% (night) / 18% (rotating). The 10% diabetes
and ~30% hypertension figures are correct.

**2.4 — Claim not in source (cortisol/immune).** "Night shift employees show higher cortisol
levels and poorer immune function…" The cited umbrella review presents no cortisol or
immune-marker data.

**2.5 — Claim not in source (Tucker 2003).** The paper attributes to Tucker's rest-break
review a finding about "a minute's movement and posture change" countering musculoskeletal
strain and offsetting prolonged sitting. Tucker 2003 covers rest-break scheduling, accident
risk, fatigue and performance. Re-attribute — Owen et al. (2010) already covers the sitting half.

**2.6 — Source mischaracterized: Domínguez (2017).** Cited to justify a design that "draws on
existing knowledge rather than on the collection of new data." Domínguez defines design-based
research as designing innovations and *testing them in naturalistic settings* — inherently
data-collecting. This is the citation holding up the study's entire no-user-testing stance,
so it is the one a methodologist attacks first.

**2.7 — Source mischaracterized: Deffuant et al. (2024).** Described as bias "in the direction
of the evaluator's interest." The paper's central claim is the reverse: *"Its cause is purely
statistical and it has nothing to do with any motivation related to self."* The "without
awareness" half is correct. Cited twice — Ch. III safeguards and Ch. IV limitations.

**2.8 — Unquoted verbatim text.** "safety declines over successive night shifts, with
increasing hours on duty, and between successive rest breaks" is word-for-word from Folkard &
Tucker's (2003) abstract, presented as the paper's own prose. Appears twice.

## 5. Rubric accuracy and bias

### The structural finding

`vitest.config.js` is `environment: "node"`, `include: ["src/**/*.test.js"]`. There is no
jsdom, no axe-core, no testing-library, no `.jsx` test and no CI. **Nothing in the suite renders
anything.** Every accessibility test — `care-a11y`, `controls-a11y`, `navstate-a11y`,
`reflection-a11y`, `focus`, `announce` — is a string match against the raw text of `App.jsx`,
e.g. `expect(bodyOf(...)).toContain("aria-pressed={active}")`. That is a source lint, not
confirmation of shipped behaviour, and it is the load-bearing evidence under several band 3s.
